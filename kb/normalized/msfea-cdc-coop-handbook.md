# MSFEA CO-OP (Cooperative Education) Handbook

This is the authoritative reference for the MSFEA Cooperative Education (CO-OP)
program. It covers what CO-OP is, the policies, the FEAA 500 course, student
eligibility and application, deliverables, evaluation, and information for
employers.

## What CO-OP is

Cooperative education ("co-op") is a program in which students combine their
studies with one or more official **paid** work terms within their area of
study. The difference between a co-op work term and an internship is duration and
pay: internships are typically short (one or two months) and can be unpaid, while
co-op terms are longer and are paid. The **MSFEA co-op work term is a minimum of
6 months**, full-time and paid.

According to the U.S. Department of Education, students with co-op experience earn
almost 8–9% more within ten years of graduation compared to students with a
graduate degree.

> **Note on duration wording:** the general definition of co-op describes "at
> least four months" as a typical co-op length, but the **MSFEA requirement is a
> minimum of 6 months**. The 6-month figure is the one that applies to MSFEA
> students.

## Motivation and benefits

- **For students:** a co-op lets students immerse themselves in an industry and
  better understand their field. A paid, extended placement builds a stronger
  sense of responsibility and makes the experience more meaningful. Research shows
  co-op participation increases student interest in learning and academic
  performance on return to campus, and leads to more satisfying career outcomes
  (Blair et al., 2004; Boudreau and Marx, 2019).
- **For employers:** access to talent and early recruitment, plus becoming part of
  MSFEA's Corporate Partnership program.

## The CO-OP program at MSFEA

The program has **two components**: Career Development Preparation, and the
Cooperative Education Experience itself.

### Career Development Preparation (modules)

The CDC provides **0-credit Career Development Modules**, delivered
asynchronously via Moodle:

1. Resume and Cover Letter writing
2. Interview preparation
3. Work placement search
4. Workplace environment and skills

These modules are **required before a student may apply for co-op positions**. If
the online modules are not ready for the first cohort, they are replaced by
workshops. The CDC also supports students with one-to-one consultations if needed
(resume review, career counseling, mock interviews).

### The Cooperative Education Experience

Students complete at least one job placement with a **minimum duration of 6
months**, associated with the **3-credit course FEAA 500**. Students may complete
(and employers may propose) multiple 6-month co-op experiences, consecutive or
not. The objectives are for students to apply and enhance technical skills;
practice professional and communication skills; be introduced to the industry;
gain a deeper understanding of their field; interact with supervisors and
colleagues; make better career decisions; and build professional connections.

### People

The program involves co-op students and co-op employers, and is administered by a
**co-op committee** made up of a Chair, a CDC Advisor, and Academic Advisors from
participating departments.

## Policies

- **Optional or required:** CO-OP is an **optional program**, open to
  undergraduate students (and to graduate students if allowed by their
  department).
- **Hours and salary:** students work **paid full-time jobs**, a minimum of 35–40
  hours per week. Pay should be higher than a stipend but slightly below a
  fresh-graduate salary. MSFEA can provide salary guidelines, but the salary is
  set by the employer.
- **Courses while on co-op:** enrolling in other courses during a co-op is **not
  allowed**, with a possible exception for a Final Year Project (FYP) or
  research-based course (499) if the department allows it and the co-op academic
  advisor (and FYP group members, for an FYP) approve — and only if it does not
  interfere with working hours.
- **Number of credits:** the co-op is a **3-credit Pass/Fail FEAA 500** course.
  Whether it counts toward academic credit, and whether it is treated as a free
  elective or a technical elective, is decided by each department. Students keep
  **full-time student status** during the co-op.
- **Tuition / co-op fees:** regular tuition is charged for the 3 credits of FEAA
  500. Students who pass FEAA 500 are **no longer required** to take the 0-credit
  Approved Experience / Professional Training course in their department (that
  course is currently billed as 1 credit).
- **Number, duration, and certification:** one placement of at least 6 months is
  required; additional co-ops (consecutive or not) are allowed if approved by the
  department and co-op advisor. A 6-month co-op should typically coincide with the
  **Summer + Fall** or **Spring + Summer** semesters. The co-op appears as a
  course on the academic transcript.
- **Timing and return to campus:** the co-op should take place in the **3rd or 4th
  year** for engineering and graphic design students, and the **4th or 5th year**
  for architecture students. Students must **return to campus for at least one
  term** after their final co-op before graduating.
- **Preparation and support:** info sessions, co-op advising weeks, on-demand
  meetings with the co-op CDC Advisor (resume review, counseling, mock
  interviews), the career development modules, access to co-op employers and
  opportunities, follow-up and feedback, and faculty mentorship and assessment.
- **Program length and graduation date:** students may take overloads to complete
  the co-op without delaying graduation, with careful planning and department
  approval. In some cases participation may delay graduation by one term (for one
  co-op).

## Information for students

### Eligibility requirements

To participate in the co-op program and enroll in FEAA 500, a student must satisfy:

1. **Degree:** be an undergraduate student in MSFEA (graduate students may be
   eligible if their department allows).
2. **Minimum GPA:** **3.3 (B+)**, required both at application to the program and
   when enrolling in FEAA 500.
3. **Minimum credits:** at least **90 completed credits** (120 for architecture
   students) when enrolling in FEAA 500 — not required at the time of applying to
   the program.
4. **Career development modules:** successfully complete the required CDC
   modules/workshops before applying to co-op positions and enrolling in FEAA 500.
5. **Department-specific requirements:** individual departments may set a higher
   minimum GPA, more credits, specific pre-requisite courses, or other
   requirements.

Applications are reviewed by the department's co-op academic advisor, who accepts
or rejects in consultation with the department or its Undergraduate Committee.

### Application and admission process

Students can apply starting at the **beginning of the Spring of their 2nd year**,
and should apply **at least one year before** the intended co-op start date.

The process has two stages:

1. **Acceptance into the program:** submit the **Co-op Student Application Form**
   and meet the co-op Academic Advisor during co-op advising week to discuss the
   application, eligibility, career interests, and course schedule. Decisions are
   made by the department's co-op academic advisor.
2. **Applying to positions:** once accepted and after completing the required
   modules, students apply to co-op positions advertised by the Career Center, or
   seek approval for an opportunity they find themselves (by submitting a
   **Proposal of Cooperative Education and Experiential Learning Form** and
   contacting the co-op CDC Advisor). Students may apply to a **maximum of 3**
   co-op opportunities, ranked by priority, but can only accept and confirm one.

Being accepted into the program does **not** guarantee a placement — students
compete for available positions. When a student receives and accepts an offer,
they sign the **Co-op Joint Agreement Form** together with the employer and the
CDC Advisor. A deadline is attached to each step, for both the "Summer + Fall" and
"Spring + Summer" tracks (see the timeline below).

### Application timeline (deadlines)

The process follows one of two tracks depending on the intake — **Summer + Fall**
or **Spring + Summer** — with the same steps offset by roughly five months.

**Student steps**

| Step | Action | Summer + Fall | Spring + Summer |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Apply to the co-op program | Jul | Feb |
| 2 | Meet your co-op Academic Advisor during advising week | Aug | Mar |
| 3 | Complete the co-op career development modules | Sep | Apr |
| 4 | Apply to advertised co-op positions via the CDC | Oct | May |
| 5 | Receive an offer | Dec | Jul |
| 6 | Sign the Co-op Joint Agreement Form | Jan | Aug |
| → | **Start of the co-op experience** | Jun | Jan |

**Employer steps**

| Step | Action | Summer + Fall | Spring + Summer |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Advertise co-op positions | ~Sep–Oct | ~Apr–May |
| 2 | Shortlist & interview eligible applicants | ~Oct–Nov | ~May–Jun |
| 3 | Extend an offer | Dec | Jul |
| 4 | Sign the Co-op Joint Agreement Form | Jan | Aug |
| → | **Start of the co-op experience** | Jun | Jan |

### The FEAA 500 course

A student who accepts an offer enrolls in **FEAA 500 in both semesters** of the
co-op position (Summer and Fall, or Spring and Summer):

- **First semester:** register **FEAA 500** (3 credits) and pay its tuition.
- **Second semester:** register **FEAA 500A** (3 credits) with **no additional tuition** (no additional tuition fees).

Full-time student status is maintained in both semesters. A student with
satisfactory performance at the end of the first co-op semester receives a grade
of **"In Progress (PR)"** on FEAA 500; the final **Pass or Fail** grade is
assigned on **FEAA 500A** at the end of the second semester.

### Attitude, behavior, and responsibilities at work

Students must maintain a highly professional attitude: punctuality and attendance
are essential, good relations with supervisors and colleagues matter, and students
represent AUB during the co-op. Students should meet regularly with their
supervisor for feedback. The co-op advisor liaises with the employer for a
**midterm site visit** (in person or online); the student must submit the co-op
progress report before the visit. During the visit, the advisor, employer, and
student discuss the student's performance. Additional site visits may be scheduled
if needed.

### Deliverables and deadlines

Students maintain an individual co-op experience file. Deliverables in FEAA 500:

**During the co-op:**

1. **Notice of Arrival Form** (submitted through Moodle) — sent during the first
   week of the co-op.
2. **One-page Co-op Progress Report** — submitted at the end of the 3rd month.

**At the end of the co-op:**

3. **Co-op Final Report** — a three-page memo describing the work completed, plus
   appendices with work samples.
4. **Student Self-Evaluation, Reflection, and Feedback Form.**
5. Additional requirements may be set by individual departments.

### Student evaluation

Satisfactory progress in the first semester earns an **"In Progress (PR)"** grade
on FEAA 500; the final **Pass/Fail** grade is assigned on FEAA 500A at the end of
the second semester. The co-op CDC and academic advisors evaluate the deliverables
against these criteria:

1. Completion of the co-op experience file.
2. Quality and relevance of the professional experience acquired.
3. Quality of the Co-op Progress and Final Reports.
4. Satisfactory student performance evaluation by the employer during the site
   visit.
5. Satisfactory **Final Student Performance Evaluation** by the employer.
6. Completion of the Student Self-Evaluation, Reflection, and Feedback Form.
7. Additional criteria set by individual departments.

Students may be asked to revise or repeat any deliverable that does not meet
department expectations.

### Requirements to pass the co-op course (summary)

Submit a one-page progress report; have a successful midterm site visit; obtain a
favorable Final Student Performance Evaluation Form from the job supervisor;
submit the final report (three-page memo plus appendices); and complete the
Student Self-Evaluation, Reflection, and Feedback Form. Departments may add
requirements (e.g. a portfolio for architecture and graphic design students).

## Information for employers

### Position requirements

Co-op positions offered to MSFEA students should satisfy:

1. An established company known for meaningful training experiences.
2. A history of relationship with AUB and MSFEA (preferred).
3. Quality and relevance of the experience, evaluated by the co-op advisors from a
   detailed job description.
4. **Paid positions** — higher than a stipend and slightly below a full-time
   fresh-graduate salary.
5. Duration of **35–40 hours per week for 6 months**.
6. Any additional requirements set by individual departments.

### Employer application process

Employers submit a **Co-op Employer Application Form** with a detailed job
description. If the position is approved by the relevant department and co-op
academic advisor, the employer receives a list of eligible applicants from the
co-op CDC Advisor, then shortlists, interviews, and decides whether to extend an
offer. If an offer is accepted, the employer signs the **Co-op Joint Agreement
Form** with the student and CDC Advisor. A deadline is attached to each step for
both tracks (see the timeline above).

### Employer responsibilities during the co-op

The employer/supervisor should meet regularly with the student and be available
for the midterm site visit. At the end of the co-op, the employer evaluates the
student and submits a **Final Student Performance Evaluation Form** (stating the
dates of the position and a brief description of the work), and finally a **Co-op
Employer Experience Feedback Form**.

## CO-OP FAQs

**Q: Is CO-OP mandatory?**
A: No, it is an **optional program** students can choose to enroll in.

**Q: What GPA do I need for co-op?**
A: A minimum GPA of **3.3 (B+)**.

**Q: What are the student eligibility criteria for CO-OP?**
A: Undergraduate student in MSFEA (Engineering, Architecture, or Graphic Design);
minimum GPA of 3.3 (B+); at least 90 completed credits (120 for architecture) when
enrolling in FEAA 500; completion of the CDC Career Development Modules; plus any
department-specific requirements.

**Q: Is CO-OP associated with a course?**
A: Yes — the 3-credit Pass/Fail course **FEAA 500** (and **FEAA 500A** in the
second semester), which you register for every semester you are on co-op.

**Q: How many CO-OP opportunities can I apply to?**
A: Up to a **maximum of 3**, ranked by priority, but you can only accept and
confirm one.

**Q: Can I take other courses while on CO-OP?**
A: No, except possibly Final Year Project (FYP) or a 499 research course, if your
department allows it and it doesn't interfere with working hours.

**Q: When can I go on CO-OP?**
A: In the 3rd or 4th year for Engineering/Graphic Design students, and the 4th or
5th year for Architecture students. You must return to campus for at least one
term afterward.

**Q: When should I apply to the CO-OP program?**
A: At least one year before your intended start date (you can begin applying in the
Spring of your 2nd year).

**Q: Do CO-OP course credits count toward my degree?**
A: This is decided by your department. You remain a full-time student during co-op
regardless.
