# Internship (Approved Experience) Report Templates and Rubrics

The Approved Experience has three graded deliverables that build on each other:
the **Progress Report** (a mid-experience checkpoint), the **Final Training
Report** (the primary evidence of a completed, meaningful experience), and the
**Final Presentation**. Each template below lists the required content; each
rubric shows how it is assessed. Assessment is Pass/Fail, so no percentage
breakdowns are assigned.

## 1. Progress Report Template (3–5 pages)

**Purpose.** The Progress Report is a mid-experience check-in that allows the department to verify that:

- the Approved Experience is relevant and meaningful;
- the student is actively engaged in appropriate work and responsibilities;
- technical, professional, and disciplinary learning is taking place; and
- the student is making satisfactory progress toward completing the Approved Experience.

### Cover Page

Include:

- Student Name
- ID Number
- Department
- Organization Name
- Approved Experience Start Date
- Approved Experience End Date
- Progress Report Submission Date
- Supervisor Name and Title

### Organization Overview

Briefly describe the organization; its industry, services, products, or areas of practice; your department, unit, or team; and your supervisor's role. (Approximately ½ page)

### Approved Experience Role and Responsibilities

Describe your position or role; responsibilities assigned to date; typical tasks performed; and teams, departments, clients, or stakeholders with whom you interact. (Approximately 1 page)

### Progress and Contributions

Describe:

- projects, assignments, or activities completed to date;
- tasks performed and contributions made;
- tools, software, equipment, methods, or processes used; and
- examples that demonstrate your involvement and responsibilities.

(Approximately 1–2 pages)

### Learning and Professional Development

Discuss the technical, professional, and disciplinary learning gained to date. Provide specific examples of:

- skills developed;
- knowledge acquired;
- workplace practices observed;
- communication and teamwork experiences; and
- insights gained about your profession or field.

### Remaining Goals and Next Steps

Describe work still to be completed; learning goals for the remainder of the Approved Experience; anticipated challenges; and any support needed from the supervisor, employer, or department to achieve these goals.

### Progress Report Rubric

| Category | On Track | Needs Attention |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Experience Context | Provides sufficient description of the organization, team, supervisor, and role to understand the work environment. | Description is incomplete, unclear, or does not adequately explain the Approved Experience context. |
| Work Progress and Engagement | Clearly describes tasks, projects, responsibilities, and contributions completed to date, demonstrating active participation. | Limited description of work completed or insufficient evidence of engagement. |
| Learning and Skill Development | Identifies technical, professional, and/or disciplinary learning supported by specific examples. | Learning outcomes are unclear, superficial, or unsupported by examples. |
| Reflection and Future Plans | Demonstrates thoughtful reflection on experiences to date and identifies realistic goals and next steps. | Reflection is minimal or future goals are unclear or missing. |
| Communication Clarity | Report is clear, organized, complete, and professional. | Significant organizational, formatting, or writing issues impede understanding. |

Students receiving a rating of Needs Attention in two or more categories may be asked to revise and resubmit the report.

## 2. Final Training Report Template (8–15 pages)

**Purpose.** The Final Approved Experience Report serves as the primary evidence that the student successfully completed a meaningful professional experience and achieved the learning objectives of the Approved Experience course. The report should emphasize analysis, application, learning, and reflection rather than simply describing activities. Students should demonstrate how they applied knowledge from their academic program, contributed to their organization, and developed professionally throughout the experience.

### Cover Page

Include: Course Number and Title; Student Name; ID Number; Department; Organization Name; Approved Experience Location; Approved Experience Dates; Submission Date.

### Executive Summary

Provide a concise overview of the Approved Experience, including the purpose of the experience; primary responsibilities; major projects or contributions; technical, professional, and disciplinary learning outcomes; and overall conclusions. (Approximately 250–500 words)

### Table of Contents

### 1. Introduction

Describe the organization; the purpose and objectives of the Approved Experience; your department, unit, or team; and an overview of your role and responsibilities. (Approximately 1 page)

### 2. Organization and Work Environment

Describe:

- the organizational structure;
- the team structure and key collaborators;
- relevant stakeholders, clients, or partners; and
- the role of engineering, design, architecture, planning, computing, or other disciplinary expertise within the organization.

### 3. Major Projects and Technical Contributions

This should be the largest section. For each major project, assignment, or responsibility, discuss:

- **Project Overview** — objectives; background and context; and project significance.
- **Your Responsibilities** — specific role; tasks performed; and contributions made.
- **Methods, Tools, and Approaches** — software, equipment, or technologies used; analytical, engineering, design, computational, or creative methods applied; and processes or workflows followed.
- **Outcomes** — results achieved; deliverables produced; lessons learned; and impact on the organization, project, team, or client.

### 4. Application of Engineering, Design, and Disciplinary Knowledge

Discuss how knowledge gained through your academic program informed your work. Examples may include:

- engineering principles;
- scientific concepts;
- mathematics;
- design methodologies;
- computational tools;
- data analysis techniques;
- modeling approaches;
- fabrication methods; and
- discipline-specific knowledge relevant to your field.

Students should explain not only what they did, but why particular approaches, methods, or decisions were appropriate.

### 5. Professional Skills and Workplace Learning

Discuss your development in areas such as teamwork and collaboration; communication; project management; leadership; professionalism; workplace expectations; time management; and interactions with supervisors, colleagues, clients, or stakeholders. Provide specific examples where possible.

### 6. Challenges, Problem Solving, and Adaptation

Discuss technical, organizational, ethical, or resource-related challenges encountered; constraints that affected your work; problem-solving strategies used; solutions implemented; and knowledge or skills acquired independently to address challenges.

### 7. Reflection and Career Development

Reflect on the most important lessons learned; how the experience influenced your career goals; strengths you discovered or strengthened; areas for continued growth and development; and how the experience contributed to your understanding of the profession.

### 8. Conclusions and Recommendations

Summarize the overall value of the Approved Experience; the most significant technical and professional learning outcomes; insights gained about the profession or industry; and recommendations for improving future Approved Experience opportunities. Students may also identify gaps between academic preparation and workplace expectations and suggest ways to strengthen student preparation.

### References

Include all sources referenced in the report using a consistent citation style. All referenced information, figures, tables, images, and external materials should be properly cited in-text and in the list.

### Appendices

May include drawings; calculations; screenshots; code samples; project documentation; technical outputs; employer documentation; and notice of completion. All appendices should be referenced within the report.

### Final Approved Experience Report Rubric

| Criteria | Pass | Revision Required | Fail |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Organization, Format, and Professional Writing | Report is professionally organized and formatted. Required sections are present. Writing is clear, coherent, and appropriate for a professional audience. Figures, tables, references, and appendices are effectively integrated. Minor errors do not affect readability. | Organization, formatting, writing quality, references, or supporting materials require improvement. Some required elements may be incomplete or inconsistently presented. | Report lacks required sections, is poorly organized, contains significant writing problems, or does not meet basic professional standards. |
| Description and Analysis of Experience | Clearly describes the organization, role, responsibilities, projects, activities, and contributions. Demonstrates meaningful participation and understanding through detailed examples and analysis. | Experience is described but important details, examples, or analysis are limited or incomplete. | Description is minimal, unclear, incomplete, or does not demonstrate meaningful participation. |
| Application of Engineering, Design, or Disciplinary Knowledge | Clearly demonstrates how disciplinary knowledge, methods, tools, technologies, analyses, or problem-solving approaches were applied. Provides specific and relevant examples. | Application of disciplinary knowledge is mentioned but lacks sufficient detail or explanation. | Little or no evidence of disciplinary knowledge being applied or understood. |
| Professional Learning and Growth | Demonstrates meaningful learning throughout the experience. Clearly explains skills developed, knowledge acquired, professional practices learned, and contributions to personal and professional growth. | Learning experiences are discussed but lack depth, reflection, or specific examples. | Little or no evidence of learning, growth, adaptation, or professional development. |
| Professional Practice and Broader Impact | Demonstrates awareness of professional considerations relevant to the experience, such as safety, sustainability, regulations, standards, quality, ethics, economic considerations, environmental impacts, societal impacts, or workplace responsibilities. Provides thoughtful reflection on the broader context of professional work. | Professional considerations are mentioned but discussion is superficial or lacks clear connection to the experience. | No meaningful discussion of professional practice or broader professional considerations. |

The assessment criteria align with MSFEA learning objectives and relevant accreditation outcomes: Communication; Application of disciplinary knowledge; Professional learning and lifelong learning; and Professional responsibility and awareness of broader impacts. Because Approved Experience is assessed on a Pass/Fail basis, percentage breakdowns are not assigned.

## 3. Final Presentation

**Purpose.** The Final Presentation evaluates the student's ability to communicate professional experiences, disciplinary learning, and major contributions clearly and concisely. In no more than five slides, students should highlight the most significant aspects of their Approved Experience, including responsibilities, major contributions, application of disciplinary knowledge, challenges encountered, and key learning outcomes.

### Presentation Requirements

- 3–5 minutes in length
- Maximum of 5 slides
- Recorded or delivered in person, as determined by the department
- Use visuals strategically to support communication and audience understanding
- Respect employer confidentiality and avoid sharing proprietary or restricted information

### Suggested Slide Structure

- **Slide 1 — Approved Experience Overview:** organization; position or role; duration; and primary responsibilities.
- **Slide 2 — Major Project or Contribution:** project, assignment, or contribution; objectives; your role; and significance of the work.
- **Slide 3 — Engineering/Design Knowledge Applied:** tools, software, technologies, or methods used; engineering, design, or disciplinary knowledge applied; and examples of analysis, design, decision-making, or problem solving.
- **Slide 4 — Key Learning Outcomes:** technical learning; professional and workplace learning; communication and teamwork experiences; and challenges overcome.
- **Slide 5 — Reflection and Takeaways:** the most valuable lesson learned; career insights gained; impact of the experience on your professional development; and advice for future students.

### Final Presentation Rubric

| Criteria | Pass | Revision Required | Fail |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Content and Organization | Presentation clearly explains the Approved Experience, including the organization, role, responsibilities, contributions, challenges, and learning outcomes. Information is logically organized and easy to follow. | Most required information is present, but clarity, detail, organization, or flow require improvement. | Presentation is incomplete, poorly organized, or difficult to understand. |
| Application of Disciplinary Knowledge | Clearly explains how disciplinary knowledge, methods, tools, technologies, analyses, design approaches, or problem-solving strategies were applied during the Approved Experience. Appropriate examples are provided. | Technical or disciplinary work is mentioned but explanation is limited or unclear. | Little or no evidence of disciplinary knowledge application. |
| Professional Learning and Growth | Clearly identifies skills developed, challenges encountered, lessons learned, and professional growth resulting from the experience. Reflection is supported by specific examples. | Learning and growth are discussed but lack depth or supporting examples. | Minimal discussion of learning, development, or professional growth. |
| Visual Communication and Presentation Design | Slides and visuals effectively support communication. Figures, diagrams, charts, images, and graphics are clear, relevant, professional, and enhance audience understanding. | Visuals are present but may be crowded, unclear, inconsistently formatted, or only partially support communication. | Visuals are absent, ineffective, difficult to read, or detract from communication. |
| Presentation Requirements and Professionalism | Presentation follows all stated requirements and demonstrates preparation, professionalism, and effective communication. | Minor deviations from requirements or professionalism expectations. | Significant deviations from requirements or lack of professional quality. |

The assessment criteria align with MSFEA learning objectives and relevant accreditation outcomes: Communication; Application of disciplinary knowledge; Professional learning and lifelong learning; and Professional presentation and communication skills. Because Approved Experience is assessed on a Pass/Fail basis, percentage breakdowns are not assigned.
